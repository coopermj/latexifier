# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

A pastor (primarily the author) and a small circle of followers — people engaged with scripture at a serious level who will read and annotate this document on a Remarkable Paper Pro. They are not casual readers. They arrive at the document with intent to study, and they will write in the margins.

## Product Purpose

To produce a typeset PDF — sermon notes elevated into a document worthy of the material. The output combines the pastor's outline, scripture text, Greek interlinear, and patristic/historical commentary into a coherent, well-organized whole. The web interface is a means to that end. The PDF is the product.

The device target is the Remarkable Paper Pro: 11.8" e-ink, 229 PPI, grayscale. No color reaches the reader. Weight, scale, whitespace, and proportion are the only tools.

## Brand Personality

Scholarly. Precise. Serious.

## Anti-references

- Church bulletin aesthetic: clip art borders, drop shadows, stock photography, centered Comic Sans
- Generic PDF template look: Times New Roman 12pt, 1-inch margins, no visual hierarchy
- PowerPoint handout style: bullet-heavy, low information density, large fonts for a room
- Modern "clean" SaaS docs: excessive whitespace, thin fonts, lots of gray — too cold for devotional material
- Study Bible populism: colored call-out boxes, sidebars, pull quotes in rounded rectangles

## Design Principles

1. **Typography carries everything.** On e-ink there is no color, no shadow, no gradient. Weight contrast, size ratio, and leading are the sole instruments of hierarchy — use them with precision and commitment.

2. **The page is a unit of attention.** Each sub-point owns a page. That structure should feel like a deliberate compositional choice, not a pagination accident. Margins, whitespace, and anchor placement should make each page feel resolved.

3. **Generous margins are functional.** The Remarkable is an annotation device. The inner and outer margins are workspace. Design them wide enough to be used, not just decorative.

4. **Serif/sans contrast is meaningful.** The body and scripture in a humanist serif; commentary in Helvetica Neue. That distinction signals register — "this is the pastor's material / this is the tradition speaking" — without color.

5. **Ornament earns its place.** If a rule, dingbat, or decorative element appears, it does structural work (separates sections, anchors hierarchy). No decoration for its own sake.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

E-ink rendering. High contrast is non-negotiable — all text must be effectively black on white. Avoid any tints, grays, or reduced-opacity text that e-ink will render as illegible smear. No color-dependent information.
